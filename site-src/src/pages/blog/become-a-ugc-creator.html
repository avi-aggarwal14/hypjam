what is a ugc creator (and why followers don't matter)

a ugc creator films short, native-style video content for brands — testimonials, unboxings, demos, "get ready with me" integrations — that the brand then runs as paid ads or organic posts. you're not being hired to post to your own following; most of what you shoot never touches your own account. the brand pays for the raw footage (and often the usage rights), then runs it themselves.

that's why having zero followers is genuinely fine. what brands are actually buying is: can you deliver a clean, well-lit, well-paced video that sounds like a real person talking, on time, to a brief. that's a skill you can start building today with nothing but a phone.

the ugc creator starter kit checklist

you don't need much to start pitching for paid work:

  • [ ] a smartphone with a decent camera (most phones from the last few years are fine)
  • [ ] a simple lighting setup — a ring light or even a well-placed window
  • [ ] a lapel mic or clean built-in audio (bad audio is the fastest way to get a brief rejected)
  • [ ] a tripod or phone stand — handheld only really works for specific formats
  • [ ] a free editing app (capcut is the industry default) for captions and trims
  • [ ] 3-5 self-directed sample videos covering a few different formats
  • [ ] a simple one-page portfolio or media kit (a notion page or slides link is fine)
  • [ ] a way to invoice, even a basic template, once work starts coming in

that's genuinely the whole list. nothing on it costs more than about £100-£150 all in, and you probably already own most of it.

how ugc creator rates actually work

rates vary a lot depending on usage rights, whether the brand wants to whitelist your handle, and your experience — treat the numbers below as a general guide, not a guarantee:

single video, organic use only£75-£150brand can post it, no paid ad usage

bundled package (3-5 videos)£300-£800per-video rate usually drops a bit at volume

a few things move the number in practice: how many revisions are included, whether you're following a script or writing your own, how long the brand can use the footage for, and whether it's a one-off or a repeat relationship. always agree usage length and platforms in writing before you shoot.

building a portfolio with zero client experience

this is the bit that stops most people before they start — "how do i show i can do this if nobody's paid me yet." the answer is: shoot spec content.

pick 3-5 products you already own or can pick up cheaply, and shoot them as if a real brand had briefed you — a problem-solution hook, an unboxing, a quick review. cover a few different formats so brands can see range, not just one trick. keep each one under 60 seconds, caption it, and make it genuinely watchable, not just "content."

put these into a simple portfolio: a short intro clip of you to camera saying who you are and what you shoot, the sample videos themselves, and a line on your niche if you have one (beauty, food, home, fitness — a niche makes you easier to cast, not more limited). 27 UGC hooks that stop the scroll (with scripts) is worth a read for the kind of opening lines that actually earn a few extra seconds of watch time.

where to find ugc creator jobs (uk and beyond)

a few reliable routes in:

  • agency rosters — agencies like hypjam cast from a pool of vetted creators for ongoing client briefs, which tends to be steadier work than chasing one-off gigs
  • ugc marketplaces — platforms built specifically to match brands and creators for paid ugc work
  • direct outreach — pitching small-to-mid dtc brands directly, especially ones you already use and rate
  • facebook and discord communities — plenty of active ugc creator groups sharing briefs and job posts

if you're searching "ugc creator jobs uk," worth knowing early: most ugc work is self-employed, so you'll want to register as a sole trader and sort your own invoicing and tax from your first paid brief onward. it's simple, but easy to forget once a first booking lands and the excitement kicks in.

faq

do i need followers to become a ugc creator?

no. ugc creator work is paid for the video itself, not for posting to your own audience — brands are buying the footage, not your reach. a strong portfolio matters far more than a follower count.

how much can you realistically make as a ugc creator?

it depends entirely on volume and rates, but as a rough guide, creators working with a handful of regular brands each month at the ranges above can build this into solid part-time or even full-time income over time. treat early numbers as a ramp-up, not a ceiling.

do i need professional camera equipment to start?

no — a recent smartphone, a basic light source and clean audio cover almost every brief you'll get early on. brands specifically want the native, phone-shot look; an overly "produced" video can actually work against you.

what's the fastest way to land my first paid ugc job?

build 3-5 spec videos, put them in a simple portfolio, and apply to a few agency rosters and marketplaces in parallel rather than waiting on one reply. join the creator roster hypjam's roster as one of those applications.
