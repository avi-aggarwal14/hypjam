01 · Why UGC
Why creator videos work
Authenticity
People trust people
A brand saying "track by text" is a claim. A real person texting a photo of their lunch and reading the macros that come back is proof.
Show, do not tell
The demo fits in two seconds
A photo goes into a blue bubble and the numbers come back. No app to open and no screens to explain, which is exactly what a short video is good at.
Objections
Answer "I never stick with tracking" out loud
It is the first thing most people think. A creator texting every meal for a week, on camera, answers it better than any feature list.
A reason to act
Free, and no download
Your site says it plainly: free on iPhone, no download needed to get started. Creators can say both in the first seconds, and viewers can send their first text before the video ends.
Fuel for ads
Your best ads come from creators
The strongest creator videos become your Meta ads. We test 8 openings per script, so the budget follows what people actually watch.
Doesn't feel like an ad
Food videos are already the feed
A Biblo video looks like a normal "what I eat in a day", so people watch it instead of scrolling past. It is still labelled as an ad, it just does not feel like one.
02 · What we learned
Where Biblo is today
The product
A personal AI fitness agent you text. Send a meal photo or describe what you ate, and Biblo replies with estimated calories, protein, carbs and fat. Add details, ask what you have eaten today, all in one iMessage conversation.
The offer
Free on iPhone, with no download needed to get started. Tap Text Biblo and send your first message. Android users can join a waitlist, and a companion app for progress and plans is planned.
The team
Led by founder and CEO Manil Lakabi, who worked on the Gen AI team at Scale AI and the FRL team at Meta. A YC Fall 2024 company: your About page says the team built AI tools that create and use software, and Biblo is now your product and public brand.
Already in motion
  • "Introducing Biblo" went up on your blog on 21 October.
  • New pages on how calorie tracking by text works and on what an AI fitness agent is.
  • Terms, privacy policy and a consumer health data notice, all updated this month.
  • Your YC page sets the next step: ordering food that fits your goals and planning workouts around your schedule.
What that tells us
The product is the demo: one photo in, one text back. Manil has written that a great agent should want less interface and less attention, and iMessage is the least interface there is. What would help now is real people showing that moment on their own meals, every week, with hooks that are tested.
The timing
Biblo went public on 21 October. The run from now to January, through Thanksgiving and the holidays, is the window to find the hooks and creators that work before the month when the most people start a new routine.
Sources: heybiblo.com (home, About, How it works, AI fitness agent, Introducing Biblo, Terms and Privacy), the YC company page, and @lakabimanil_ posts on X (24 Sep and 7 Oct 2026), all read 27 Oct 2026.
03 · The category
Putting numbers on food already travels
People already watch other people put numbers on their food. The only question is where the numbers come from.
Photo logging
A proven category
Cal AI, the photo calorie app, passed 15 million downloads in under two years, and MyFitnessPal bought it in a deal that closed in December 2025 (TechCrunch).
Creators
It reaches people through creators
Cal AI's promo code sits in the bio of a gym creator with 4.5M followers, and his Cal AI tagged post on 23 October did 124K plays. Creator partnerships are one way this category gets found.
On screen
The numbers are the content
"Full day of eating" posts type the day's totals into the caption: one did 772K plays. A barista's drink with "approximate macros" did 171K. Viewers want the numbers.
Where Biblo sits
The best-known trackers are apps you download and open. Your site puts Biblo somewhere else: one conversation in Messages, where a lunch photo and "I only ate half" are just texts.
The opening for Biblo
Photo logging already sells through creators, and "what I eat in a day" already fills the feed. In the videos we read, tracking happens inside an app. We did not find creators showing it in an iMessage thread, the one screen every iPhone viewer knows at a glance. That lane is open.
Sources: TechCrunch (2 Mar and 24 Sep 2026), heybiblo.com, @efewissie's TikTok bio and post (23 Oct 2026), and the live TikTok posts in the Proof frame, all read 27 Oct 2026.
04 · The objection
"I never stick with tracking": the objection to win
Anyone who has tried tracking knows the moment it starts to feel like homework. The answer belongs in the videos: a meal, a text, the numbers back, in a few seconds.
What you already say
  • Food tracking that works like texting
  • One conversation instead of turning every meal into a form
  • A photo, or a sentence like "two eggs, toast and a banana"
  • Ask what you have eaten today, any time
  • Free on iPhone, no download to get started
Just a text
A detail goes in the same thread, the way you would tell a friend: "I only ate half the rice", "there was dressing on the side". On camera, logging looks exactly like texting, because it is.
Nothing to install
No download and no app store page to film around. The first frame of every video can be the Messages app, which every iPhone viewer already knows.
How we use it
Script A logs a whole day in one thread and asks for the total at night. Script C is a 7-day "texting my meals" challenge for people who never stuck with tracking.
Keep it healthy
Food is shown as it is eaten, never as something to earn or burn. No weigh-ins, no before and after, no talk of cutting foods out.
What creators never say
No weight-loss, medical or body-change promises. Numbers are read as Biblo sends them, as estimates, which is your own word. Nothing your site does not say Biblo does.
05 · Audience
Who to sell to first
Start here
People who already count protein
Gym-goers, recipe creators and lifters over 50 who know their numbers and write them down. Biblo turns the log they already keep into a text. Formats A and D.
Start here
Busy people who eat out
Desk lunches, takeout bowls, a coffee order between meetings. Snap it, send it, keep walking. Your own site's example is lunch as a photo. Formats A and B.
Broadest reach
People who tried tracking and stopped
A wide audience, and the one your site speaks to: tracking that works like texting. A week-long challenge lets them see it for themselves. Format C.
Long game
Shift workers
Nurses between patients, early starts and late finishes, days with no room for an app. One text fits a five minute break. Formats B and C.
Where we would start
People who already count protein and busy people who eat out, on iPhone in the US first. Biblo is iMessage on iPhone today and your terms say the service is offered in the US first, so every creator on this board posts from the US.

Ten weeks to January
You introduced Biblo on 21 October. The next ten weeks run through Thanksgiving and the holidays into January, the month when the most people start a new routine. Hooks tested now are proven by then.
Now to mid December
First creator videos on Formats A and B go live. The best openings run as Meta partnership ads to heybiblo.com, and the hooks that win get new creators.
Thanksgiving and the holidays
Real holiday plates, texted with curiosity, never guilt. Format B's "guess the protein" works at a family table, with everyone's OK to be on camera.
January
Format C's "7 days of texting my meals" for people starting fresh, built on the hooks that already won in November and December.

Four formats for Biblo
A · Sign-ups
What I eat in a day, by text

A creator holds up a real meal (a takeout bowl, a coffee order, a gas station run) and guesses the protein out loud. Then texts the photo to Biblo and reads the reply. Viewers guess in the comments.

7 days of texting my meals
For people who never stuck with tracking. One meal a day on camera, all in the same thread, day 1 to day 7. It ends with a scroll back through the week. Challenges give people a reason to follow.
