how much do ugc ads cost in 2026? rates, packages, what you get

ugc ad costs in 2026 explained: typical UK/US creator rates, a package price comparison table, and the factors that push your budget up or down.

5 min readupdated 2026-09-06by the hypjam studio

short answer: a single ugc video typically costs $150–$400 (£120–£320) for content-only usage, rising to $400–$1,200+ once you add whitelisting, extra hooks or a rush turnaround. full-service packages bundling several creators, scripts and edits usually land between $1,500 and $8,000 a month. below: typical rate ranges, a package comparison, and what actually moves the price.

the short answer: typical ugc rate ranges

a quick note before the numbers: everything here is a typical range, not a price list. a lumen skin serum video and a driftwear haul video won't cost the same, seasoned creators charge more than newcomers, and every agency (hypjam included) packages things differently. use these to sense-check a quote, not to write one.

per-video rates (content only, no paid usage)

  • new or nano creators: $100–$200 / £80–£160 per video
  • experienced or niche creators: $200–$450 / £160–£350 per video
  • specialist or high-production work (beauty macro shots, food, tech unboxing): $400–$800+ / £320–£650+

these figures cover a single finished video: brief, film, basic edit, one round of revisions. they don't include the right to run the video as a paid ad.

add whitelisting or spark ads usage rights

running a creator's video as a paid ad through tiktok spark ads or meta partnership ads (whitelisting) is a separate licence on top of the content fee. typical uplift: 50–150% on top of the base rate, or a flat usage fee of $150–$500 per video for around three months of paid use, more for exclusivity or a longer term. we go deeper on how that pricing actually works in spark ads, whitelisting and usage rights: the ugc legal bits explained.

multi-hook bundles

buying 3–5 hook variations from a single shoot (same footage, different opening lines and edits) typically costs $500–$1,500 per creator. you're paying for extra scripting and edit time, not a full reshoot, so it's usually the cheapest way to get more testable ads out of one booking.

what actually drives the price

usage rights and whitelisting

this is the single biggest lever. a video that only ever gets posted to the creator's own account is worth far less than one you can run as a paid ad, target with your own budget, and put your brand name behind. longer usage terms, whitelisting on multiple platforms, and exclusivity (the creator can't make similar content for a competitor) all add cost.

number of hooks and variations

one video with one hook is cheap. one video re-cut into five different opening lines to test against each other is more expensive, but usually cheaper per-asset than five separate shoots — which is exactly why hook testing is where most of the budget should go once a concept is working. for ideas on what to actually test, see 27 ugc hooks that stop the scroll (with scripts).

creator tier, niche and production complexity

a creator experienced at ugc (as opposed to organic influencing) who can act, deliver lines naturally and shoot clean phone footage will charge more than someone new to it — and rightly so, because retakes cost you money in wasted turnaround. specialist niches (skincare, supplements, tech) and formats needing extra kit (macro lenses, overhead food rigs) also sit at the higher end.

turnaround time

rushing a creator from brief to delivered footage in 48 hours instead of two weeks usually carries a rush fee, because it means bumping other bookings.

scripting, direction and revisions

a tightly written script with a proper hook, and a round or two of directed revisions, takes more producer time than "here's the product, make something up." it's also the difference between an ad that converts and one that doesn't, so it's rarely worth cutting.

exclusivity

asking a creator not to make similar content for a competing brand for a set period costs more than a non-exclusive booking, roughly in line with how competitive the category is.

how to budget for your first ugc campaign

  • start with a starter-style package and a small paid media test budget, not a huge production spend
  • put more of the budget into hooks and variations than into "one perfect video" — you don't know which angle will work until you test it
  • budget the whitelisting fee separately from the content fee so you're not caught out when a video actually performs and you want to run it as an ad
  • brief creators properly the first time; a bad brief is the most expensive mistake in ugc, because you pay for a reshoot either way — see how to brief a ugc creator so the first cut is the final cut
  • if you'd rather someone else run the casting, scripting and editing end to end, that's what an agency package is for — hire hypjam a team to handle it

faq

is ugc cheaper than influencer marketing? usually, yes, per asset — you're paying for content and (optionally) usage rights, not for reach or a follower count. the trade-off is that ugc doesn't come with an audience attached, so you need your own paid media budget to get it in front of people. we compare the two properly in ugc vs influencer marketing: which one actually sells.

do ugc creators charge per video or per day? most charge per video or per deliverable, with the rate scaling by usage rights, hook count and turnaround. day rates show up more often for creators shooting multiple concepts back-to-back in one session, or for agency-run shoot days.

what's included in a whitelisting fee? typically the licence to run the creator's video as a paid ad from your own ad account for an agreed period (often three months), sometimes bundled with the right to boost it as a spark ad on tiktok. it doesn't usually include reshoots if the ad underperforms — that's a separate conversation.

how many ugc videos do i need per month? enough to keep testing. most brands running paid social seriously aim for at least 8–15 new videos or hooks a month so they can rotate creative before it fatigues — fewer than that and you're often running the same one or two ads until they stop working. if you're a creator wondering where you'd fit into that volume, join the roster hypjam's creator network.
