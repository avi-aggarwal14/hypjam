A growth breakdown for Moises
Why we made this

On 21 October you announced the SoundCloud partnership, with Moises now used by 80 million musicians, after Moises Studio in September and Moi in October. And on the phone, Video Recording already lets a player mute their part, record over the track and post the take to socials.

01 · Why UGC
Why creator videos work
Authenticity
People trust players
A store page says Moises separates any song. A drummer muting the drums and playing the song himself is proof.
Show, do not tell
The mute button is the demo
Moises's moment is one tap: a part drops out of the song and the player fills the gap. A short video shows that in the first second.
Objections
Answer the AI question with playing
Every video is a person playing. Moises takes a part away so a human can play it, which answers "is AI replacing musicians?" better than any statement.
A real moment
New instruments arrive in December
Black Friday is 27 November, and the holidays put guitars, drum kits and keyboards in new hands. Creators showing how to start give new players a reason to install Moises on day one.
Fuel for ads
Creator videos become your ads
Each creator video doubles as a partnership ad. Eight openings per script means the budget goes where viewers actually stay.
Doesn't feel like an ad
Looks like a normal cover
So people watch it to the end instead of scrolling past. It is still labelled as an ad, it just does not interrupt.
02 · What we learned
Where Moises is today
The product
The creative suite for musicians. Separate any song into vocals, drums, bass, guitar, keys and more (27 stem types), mute the part you play or solo the one you are learning, see the chords, change the speed and the key, and practice with a smart metronome and count-in. Video Recording films your take over the track and exports it to socials. On iPhone, iPad, Android, desktop and the web.
Just shipped
4 March: Charlie Puth joins as Chief Music Officer. 2 September: Moises Studio, a real-time collaborative music workspace in the browser. October: Moi, the production assistant in Studio. 21 October: the SoundCloud partnership. 27 October: the latest iPhone update.
The backing
80M musicians use Moises (21 October). $40M Series A for Music AI, the company behind Moises, led by Connect Ventures and monashees (January 2025). Apple's iPad App of the Year for 2024 and an Apple Design Awards finalist in 2025. 4.7 stars from more than 28K US App Store ratings, and 261K followers on Instagram.

  • A Partner Program for musicians and creators of every audience size, and #MadeWithMoises creators on your home page.

04 · The objection
"Is AI here to replace musicians?": the objection to win
Musicians are wary of AI that makes songs for you. Moises does the opposite, and the videos should show it: the app takes a part away, and a person plays it.
What you already say
  • "AI, when done right, isn't here to replace musicians." (Charlie Puth)
  • Studio is built on the principle that AI should not make music for you
  • Moises will never train on your data
  • Models are trained only on licensed materials
Human playing is the hero
In every script the star is a real take: the drummer filling the muted drums, the beginner on day one, the singer in her own key. Moises is the practice tool around it.
Built with artists
Your artists board, from Charlie Puth to Cory Henry and Eloy Casagrande, helps guide what Moises builds. Creators can say that in one line, in their own words.
How we use it
Scripts A, B and D end on the creator playing, and Script C on the creator singing. No script shows generated music in place of a performance.
Music you can post
Every paid video uses the creator's own song, a track licensed for ads, a public-domain melody they record, or the platform's commercial music library.
What creators never say
No "learn any song in a week". No promises about how fast anyone improves. Nothing your own pages do not say Moises does.

06 · The moment
From Black Friday to the New Year practice goal
Black Friday is 27 November, the holidays put new instruments under the tree, and January is when people sit down to learn. Videos filmed in November are tested before that rush.
Now to Black Friday (27 Nov)
6 to 8 cover players film Script A, one song with their part muted. The first partnership ads go live on Meta and TikTok.
December
Singers film holiday songs in their own key (Script C), and creators with a new instrument start their Day 1 series (Script B). Budget moves to the hooks that win.
January
New Year practice goals: teachers post three-step lessons (Script D), and the best Day 1 and cover videos keep running as ads.
What this needs from you
  • A Moises account for each creator, on the plan the scripts need
  • A tracking link per creator to the App Store, Google Play or moises.ai
  • A short list of tracks creators may use in paid posts, or the go-ahead to bring their own
  • One person on your team who signs off within 48 hours
Why not wait for January?
People who unwrap an instrument in December look for how to start that same week. Videos filmed in November are tested and cut by then, so the budget backs proven hooks, not first drafts.

The brief

Where: TikTok and Instagram Reels from the creator's account, plus 15s and 30s Meta and TikTok partnership ads pointing at the App Store and Google Play

Length: 30-35s master. 30s cut: shots 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7. 15s cut: shots 1, 2, 4 and 7 (hook, disclosure, the playing, CTA).

19 · Rules
Rules we never break
Disclosure
"Ad | Paid partnership with Moises" on screen, said in the first seconds, and the platform's paid-partnership label on. That is what the FTC asks for.
Who films
Adults only (18+). Creators use Moises themselves before they talk about it.

Nothing your own pages do not say Moises does. No invented numbers. We never pay for a positive opinion, only for the video.

Paid videos use songs the creator has the right to use: their own music, a track licensed for ads, a public-domain melody they record themselves, or the platform's commercial music library. We never script a label recording reposted as an ad.

Every take is the creator's own playing or singing, shown as it happened. No sped-up or overdubbed takes passed off as live, and no promises about how fast anyone learns.

Every video reviewed within 48 hours before it posts. Creators paid within 7 days of going live.
