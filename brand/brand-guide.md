# hypjam brand guide

---

## 1. name usage

- the brand name is **hypjam** — always lowercase, one word, no hyphen, no camelCase, no space. this holds everywhere: running copy, headlines, email signatures, legal footers, even the very start of a sentence.
- if a sentence would otherwise start with "hypjam" and the lowercase start reads awkwardly, rewrite the sentence rather than capitalising the name. e.g. write "at hypjam, we direct creators" instead of "Hypjam directs creators."
- do not add "the" before the name ("the hypjam" is wrong). do not pluralise, verb-ify, or possessive-apostrophe it into something clumsy ("hypjam's-ing your ads" is wrong; "hypjam's ads" is fine).
- in third-party or system contexts where lowercase truly cannot render (e.g. a legal entity name on an invoice, an all-caps signage system), use "HYPJAM" — never "Hypjam".
- do not translate, transliterate, or abbreviate the name (no "HJ", no "hyp").

## 2. descriptions

**one-line description**
> hypjam directs everyday creators into short-form ads that convert.

**30-word description**
> hypjam is a UGC agency for DTC and ecommerce brands. we brief, cast, direct, shoot, and edit everyday creators into scroll-stopping short-form ads, ready for Spark Ads and whitelisting.

*(30 words exactly)*

## 3. positioning statement

> for DTC and ecommerce brands who need short-form ads that actually convert, hypjam is the UGC agency that directs everyday creators like a production house would — brief, scripted hooks, casting, phone shoots, and edits — unlike UGC marketplaces, which hand you a list of creators and leave the direction to you. you're not hiring a creator, you're hiring the people who direct them.

## 4. voice rules

hypjam sounds like a sharp creative producer texting a client, not a marketing department. direct, warm, a little cheeky. British spelling throughout (colour, favourite, organise, whilst is allowed but optional, programme for TV/schemes, not "program"). short sentences. no exclamation-mark stacking. confident without shouting — the confidence should come from specificity, not adjectives.

### do / don't

| # | do | don't |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | do: "you're not hiring a creator, you're hiring the people who direct them." | don't: "we're a leading provider of innovative UGC solutions." |

| 3 | do: use concrete nouns — script, shot list, hook, whitelisting, Spark Ads. | don't: reach for vague nouns — synergy, ecosystem, solutions, value-add. |
| 4 | do: keep sentences short enough to read in one breath. | don't: stack three subordinate clauses into a single sentence. |

| 6 | do: use British spelling — colour, organise, favourite, licence (noun). | don't: mix in American spelling — color, organize, favorite, license (noun). |
| 7 | do: be cheeky in small doses — a dry aside, a knowing "yeah, we know" — then move on. | don't: force a joke into every sentence or reach for a pun that isn't earned. |

## 5. palette

| swatch | name | hex | usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⬛ | ground | `#0a0a0a` | primary background. the base the whole UI or deck sits on. |
| ⬛ | ground-2 | `#121212` | secondary background — sections, cards-on-ground, alternating blocks. slightly lifted off ground. |
| ⬛ | surface | `#1c1c1c` | cards, panels, input fields, anything that sits a layer above ground-2. |
| ⬛ | surface-2 | `#2a2a2a` | hover/active states on surface, nested cards, table row stripes. |
| ▪️ | line | `#3d3d3d` | borders, dividers, input outlines. never use pure white or grey for hairlines. |
| ⬜ | text | `#f4f4f4` | primary text on dark backgrounds. headlines, body copy, buttons. |
| ⬜ | text-2 | `#cfcfcf` | secondary text — subheads, captions, muted body copy. |
| ⬜ | text-3 | `#8f8f8f` | tertiary text — timestamps, placeholder copy, disabled states. |
| 🟩 | accent — vivid orange | `#ff6f1f` | the hypjam colour. CTAs, links, highlights, the play-triangle, key stats. use it to draw the eye, not to fill space — never as a background for large blocks of body text. |
| 🟧 | amber | `#f0b46a` | sparing use only — warnings, "limited" badges, a single accent moment when green is already doing the primary job nearby. never the dominant colour on a page. |

**never use pink, purple, or blue** anywhere in the brand system — no gradients, no icon tints, no chart series colours. if a chart needs a second categorical colour beyond vivid orange, reach for amber, then for greys from the palette above, before inventing a new hue.

## 6. typography

- **display typeface: Outfit**, weight 700 (bold) for headlines, hero statements, and section titles. geometric, confident, a little playful — matches the sticker-logo energy.
- **body typeface: DM Sans**, regular (400) for paragraphs and UI copy, medium (500) for buttons, labels, and emphasis inside body text.

| level | typeface / weight | size (desktop) | size (mobile) | line-height | use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display / H1 | Outfit 700 | 56–72px | 36–44px | 1.05–1.1 | hero headlines |
| H2 | Outfit 700 | 36–44px | 28–32px | 1.15 | section titles |
| H3 | Outfit 700 | 24–28px | 20–22px | 1.2 | card titles, sub-sections |
| H4 | Outfit 700 | 18–20px | 16–18px | 1.3 | small headers, labels-as-headers |
| Body large | DM Sans 400 | 18–20px | 16–17px | 1.5 | intro paragraphs, lede copy |
| Body | DM Sans 400 | 16px | 15–16px | 1.6 | default paragraph text |
| Body small / caption | DM Sans 400 | 13–14px | 13px | 1.5 | captions, metadata, footnotes |
| Button / label | DM Sans 500 | 14–16px | 14–16px | 1 | CTAs, tags, form labels |

set body copy in text-2 or text on ground/ground-2 backgrounds; never set long paragraphs in vivid orange (fails readability and looks like an alert).

## 7. logo

the hypjam mark is a sticker-style rounded square containing a the j-hook with an orange play triangle inside it — the "we speak, you watch" idea in one glyph.

**clear space**
- minimum clear space around the mark on all sides equals the height of the rounded square itself (1x unit). nothing — text, other logos, edges of the frame — should enter that zone.
- when the wordmark "hypjam" sits beside the mark, the gap between mark and wordmark is 0.5x unit, wordmark set in Outfit 700, lowercase, baseline-aligned to the visual centre of the mark.

**minimum sizes**
- digital: mark alone, minimum 24px × 24px (e.g. favicon, app icon, avatar). mark + wordmark lockup, minimum 120px wide.
- print: mark alone, minimum 10mm × 10mm. mark + wordmark lockup, minimum 30mm wide.
- below these sizes the play triangle loses legibility — don't shrink further; use the mark-only version or a simplified favicon variant instead.

**don'ts**
- don't recolour the hook anything other than black-on-sticker or the approved reverse (light bubble on dark sticker for dark-mode use).
- don't change the play triangle to any colour other than vivid orange `#ff6f1f`.
- don't stretch, skew, rotate, or add a drop shadow, bevel, or outer glow to the mark.
- don't place the mark on busy photography or low-contrast backgrounds without a solid colour plate behind it.
- don't recreate the wordmark in any typeface other than Outfit 700 lowercase.
- don't add taglines, years, or "™"-style clutter directly inside the lockup.

## 8. imagery direction

hypjam's imagery should always look like it came from a phone in a real creator's hand, never from a professional lighting rig or a stock library.

- **real creators, not models.** people who look like they'd actually be a customer of the brand being advertised — varied ages, homes, kitchens, cars, bathrooms. no white-cyc backdrops.
- **phone-shot, not cinema-shot.** vertical 9:16 as the default frame. handheld energy, natural light, a bit of grain is good — it should read as authentic, not polished to the point of looking like a TV ad.
- **captions on, always.** burned-in captions are part of the shot, not an afterthought — bold, high-contrast, positioned to survive TikTok/Instagram UI overlays (avoid the bottom 20% and top 15% of frame for key text).
- **hooks visible in the first frame.** thumbnail-worthy: a facial expression, an on-screen text hook, or a held-up product in the first still frame, since that's what most people will actually see before they decide to watch.
- **behind-the-scenes over polish.** when showing hypjam's own process (site, deck, socials), favour photos of the brief, the script on a phone screen, the shot list, the edit timeline — proof of direction, not just the finished ad.
- **avoid:** ring-light selfie aesthetics with no context, generic "influencer lifestyle" stock imagery, visible green screens, and anything that looks templated across multiple brands.
